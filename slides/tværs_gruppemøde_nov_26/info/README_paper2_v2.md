# To HIIT or Not to HIIT – Decision-Making Among Individuals With Type 2 Diabetes (T2D)

**Study 2 of the PhD project** *Optimizing Self-Management in Type 2 Diabetes: The Role of High Intensity Interval Training in Enhancing Health Outcomes*

This is a qualitative study embedded in the **LIVING trial** (NCT06091501). It complements Study 1 (the RCT of Lev Livet with and without HIIT) by exploring **why** people choose to take part, or not take part, in HIIT, and what keeps them attending.

## Project Description

### Background

Exercise improves health outcomes in T2D, but the benefits often fade because of poor adherence. HIIT is time-efficient and may make exercise easier to fit into everyday life. Little is known about how people with T2D experience the decision to commit to HIIT within a DSMP, and how they keep making that decision session after session.

### Objective

The study explores the decision-making processes of individuals with T2D as they navigate the ongoing choice of whether to participate in HIIT as part of a DSMP.

### Research Questions

- What is at stake for individuals when deciding whether to commit to HIIT?
- Which mechanisms influence their continuous decisions to attend or skip sessions?
- What concerns and perceived benefits shape the decision-making process?
- How do healthcare professionals, peers and family members influence participants' decisions?

### Study Design

This is a qualitative study embedded in the LIVING trial. It takes place at the trial site that offers Lev Livet + HIIT (LLH).

### Participants

Participants are recruited from the LIVING trial at a trial site where HiiT is optional. 

**Sample**
- ~4 participants who decline HIIT (non-participants)
- ~6 participants who take part in HIIT

### Data Collection

**Semi-structured interviews**
- Non-participants are interviewed once, after their decision.
- Participants are interviewed three times:
  - after the initial decision
  - after completing the intervention
  - one year after completing the intervention

**Diaries**
- Participants complete a short diary for each HIIT session.
- The diaries capture factors influencing attendance and reflections on taking part, and support recall in later interviews.

**Observations**
- Participant observation takes place during HIIT sessions.
- The focus is on engagement and interaction.

### Analytical Approach

**Thematic analysis** (Braun & Clarke)
- Data are coded and analysed using thematic analysis.
- The aim is to identify patterns and central themes across interviews, diaries and observations.

**Theoretical stance**
- The analysis is inductive, with no predefined theoretical framework. It focuses on understanding behaviour related to starting and maintaining physical activity.

### Ethics

The study is covered by the LIVING approval from the Danish National Center for Ethics (case no. 1-10-72-107-23).
